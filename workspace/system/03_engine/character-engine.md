# Character Engine: personality, not roles

Every named character gets all 7 layers. If a layer is empty, the character is a prop.

| Layer | Question | Example (Rennick the fox) |
|---|---|---|
| **Want** | What do they visibly chase? | Keep the diner full |
| **Fear** | What are they afraid of losing? | Being the one who ran his father's diner into the ground |
| **Flaw** | What blinds them? | Believes his own pitches |
| **Kindness** | Why can't we fully hate them? | Gives Mae credit nobody else would |
| **Secret** | What do they hide? | Never read where his father's slogans came from |
| **Voice** | Speech pattern + catchphrase + verbal tic | Fast, warm, calls everyone "friend"; "On the house." |
| **Physical tell** | A gesture the animator repeats | Flips the spatula when he's lying |

## Rules
1. **Nobody is pure.** Villains have a kindness; heroes have a flaw. The narrator has guilt.
2. **Voice test:** cover the names. If you can't tell who's speaking, rewrite.
3. **Every character wants something in the scene**, not just in life (Mae wants Pip fed before school).
4. **One tell per character** shows up every episode (recognition = series glue).
5. Personality shows in **action**, not description (Mae doesn't eat herself; we never say she's selfless).
