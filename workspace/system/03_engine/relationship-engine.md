# Relationship Engine: the emotional layer

The myth is the plot. **The relationship is why we care.** (Reference: father/son in "Stop Overthinking", husband/wife in "The Day They Sold You Love", "He's my friend, Wolf… You had to do this in front of her.")

## Relationship types (pick 1 primary + 1 secondary per episode)
| Type | Emotional stake | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Parent ↔ child | Doing right by someone you love | Mae wants Pip fed |
| Partners | Proving love / being enough | Husband forgot Valentine's |
| Friends | Loyalty vs truth | "He's my friend, Crow." |
| Debt | Owing someone who's kind to you | Mae owes Rennick's tab |
| Mentor ↔ student | Who the child will become | Pip listens to the Crow |
| Narrator ↔ villain | Old history, shared guilt | Crow painted Rennick's father's sign |

## Required emotional beats (add to the 5 acts)
1. **Act 0, show the bond in action.** One gesture of love/obligation before any myth talk.
2. **Act 1, face at stake.** Someone is embarrassed by the narrator ("Not in front of him, Crow.").
3. **Act 2, a personal interruption.** The villain's objection is personal, not factual ("My father fed this town on that plate!").
4. **Act 3, the truth costs someone something.** A confession, a lost face, a broken illusion.
5. **Act 3/4, a small act of repair.** A gesture that changes the relationship (never a lecture, never an instruction to the viewer).

## The narrator is never neutral
Every episode, the narrator has skin in the game: guilt, a debt, a soft spot. Reveal one piece of his past per episode (serial depth).
