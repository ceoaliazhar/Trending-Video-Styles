# Attention Principles: the foundation of every rule

Every rule in this system exists because of one of these principles. Styles (Quiet Wolf or any other reference) are just one way of applying them.
**Evidence strength:** Strong = well replicated · Moderate = solid but context-dependent · Mixed = popular, but results vary (use, don't over-rely).

| # | Principle | What it says | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Information gap (curiosity)** | Curiosity arises when attention focuses on a gap in what we know; we feel deprived until it closes. Small, specific gaps on familiar topics pull hardest. | Loewenstein 1994 | Strong |
| P2 | **Orienting response** | Formal features (cuts, camera changes, sudden sounds, new graphics) grab attention automatically, without conscious choice. Repeating the same feature habituates. | Lang, LC4MP 2000/2009 | Strong |
| P3 | **Limited capacity** | Viewers have a fixed processing budget. Too many features or ideas at once = overload, less remembered. | Lang 2000; Mayer (multimedia learning) | Strong |
| P4 | **Expectation violation** | Surprise (a prediction proven wrong) spikes attention and memory. | Schema-violation / prediction-error research | Strong |
| P5 | **Narrative transportation** | Being absorbed in a story (attention + imagery + emotion) shifts beliefs and can lower counter-arguing. | Green & Brock 2000 | Mixed (effects on counter-arguing vary across studies) |
| P6 | **Persuasion knowledge** | When people recognize a sales attempt, they switch to coping and resisting. | Friestad & Wright 1994 | Strong |
| P7 | **Identification** | Viewers experience a story through a character they relate to. | Cohen 2001; Busselle & Bilandzic 2009 | Moderate |
| P8 | **Loss aversion** | A loss weighs more than an equal gain. | Kahneman & Tversky 1979 | Strong |
| P9 | **Concreteness** | Specific dates, numbers and vivid images are processed and remembered better than abstractions. | Paivio dual coding; concreteness effect | Strong |
| P10 | **Isolation (Von Restorff)** | The one thing that differs from its surroundings is noticed and remembered. | Von Restorff 1933 | Strong |
| P11 | **Peak-end** | Experiences are judged mostly by their peak and their ending. | Kahneman et al. 1993 | Moderate |
| P12 | **Fluency & repetition** | Familiar, repeated elements feel easier and more trusted. | Mere exposure; processing fluency | Strong |
| P13 | **Signaling** | Cues that highlight what matters (bold keyword, title card) improve understanding. | Mayer, signaling principle | Moderate |

Avoided on purpose: the "Zeigarnik effect" (open loops remembered better) has weak replication; we rely on P1 instead.

## How to use this
1. **Writing:** for every beat, name the principle it activates. If a beat activates none, cut it.
2. **Reviewing:** when something feels flat, find the missing principle (no gap? no surprise? no stakes? overload?).
3. **New reference styles:** see `reverse-engineer.md`. Map their techniques to these principles, keep the principle, drop their signature.
