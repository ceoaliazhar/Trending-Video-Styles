# Customer Drivers: what the buyer actually cares about

A **driver** is the real reason a buyer hesitates or acts. Every myth (and so every script) must sit on one driver; otherwise the story is entertaining but sells nothing.

## The 6 driver types (reusable across categories)
| Driver | Buyer's inner question | Story move it unlocks | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Verification** | "Is this real / what it says?" | Proof test: show the number, the report, the source | P1, P4 |
| **Price** | "Am I paying too much, or getting fooled by cheap?" | Same name ≠ same thing; the cost of the shortcut | P8 |
| **Access** | "Can I get it, and who else can?" | The gate is the credibility; the offer is the door | P6, P7 |
| **Plain-language clarity** | "What is this, really?" | Explain the science in kitchen words | P3, P9 |
| **Handling / quality over time** | "Is it still good when it reaches me?" | Heat, light, time, transport as the hidden villain | P9 |
| **Insider identity** | "Am I one of the people who know?" | "Serious researchers read the batch" | P7, P12 |
Add category-specific drivers when research shows one (e.g. safety, status, speed, convenience).

## The gate
Drivers are suggested **before anything else** (no topics, myths or concepts yet), straight from competitor research, and the user approves or edits them. Present them like this:

| Driver | Evidence (what research showed) | Pull | Gap | Story | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then ask: "Approve these drivers, or change any?" and wait.

## How to find and pick them (ad mode)
1. **Collect raw drivers** from audience research (`research/R4-demand-audience.md`): fears, desires, confusions, identity, in the buyer's words.
2. **Check saturation** with competitor research (`research/R2-competitor-sites.md`, `research/R3-ad-library.md`): which drivers everyone already uses, and in what voice.
3. **Score** each: customer pull × competitor gap × story-ability (`research/selection-method.md`). Keep the top 4 as core drivers; 1–2 optional.
4. **Rule:** a high-pull driver everyone uses is still usable, but in a different voice (e.g. competitors say "99% HPLC" in lab-speak; we tell it as a plain story).
5. **Assign** one driver per script and rotate across a batch (driver 1 → case 1, driver 2 → case 2…).
6. **Save** the approved drivers in the project; every concept card names its driver.

## Organic mode
Drivers are simpler: the viewer's **emotion of being fooled** (betrayal), **curiosity about origins**, and **self-image as someone who knows**. The myth-mining lenses (`myth-mining.md`) cover these.

## Worked example (fictional brand: "Northwind Roasters", specialty coffee subscription)
Research: 5 competitor sites, Meta Ad Library, search/demand data.
- **Competitor pattern:** discount-led ads ("first bag free"), "freshly roasted" claimed by everyone, no stories, nobody explains what fresh means.
- **Approved core drivers:** 1. Verification ("Is this really fresh?" → roast date on the bag) · 2. Price (fair price per cup; seasonal offer) · 3. Access (small-batch drops, members first) · 4. Clarity (plain-language: what roast level and origin change in the cup).
- **Optional:** identity ("I'm someone who knows coffee") · handling (how to store beans).
- **Client constraint:** "too technical" → register = scientific for common folks.
- **Mapping to scripts:** Case 1 = Verification + Clarity ("The roast date is proof") · Case 2 = Access ("Why a drop sells out") · Case 3 = Price + Verification ("Cheap per bag vs cheap per cup").
