## What it makes
60–120s animated story videos/ads: a calm narrator exposes a myth; the product appears only as the proof.

## The foundation (attention principles)
| P | Principle | Use |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Information gap | Open one specific question early; close it only at the verdict. Topic must be familiar. |
| P3 | Limited capacity | One idea per line, ~6-word sentences, max 3 speakers. |
| P4 | Surprise | The villain reveals himself; a teaser fact before the flashback. |
| P6 | Persuasion knowledge | No sale words until the end; product = evidence. |
| P7 | Identification | A believer the viewer relates to. |
| P8 | Loss aversion | Stakes are a loss ("six months of research"). |
| P9 | Concreteness | Exact date, number, one vivid image. |
| P11 | Peak-end | Verdict and stake payoff land at the end. |
| P12 | Repetition | Fixed silencer ("Shh. Watch.") and ritual close. |
Full table + sources: `references/attention-principles.md`.

## The shape (copy it)
1. **Believer + stakes** (line 1)
2. **Seller's pitch** = the myth, said aloud
3. **Narrator:** "Says who?"
4. **Seller reveals himself** in his own words ("…I did.")
5. **Believer's shock**
6. **Seller attacks the narrator** ("He sells too.")
7. **Narrator:** "He's right. Don't trust me either. Shh. Watch."
8. **Flashback:** 1 date + 1 science line + 1 image (~25 words)
9. **Seller:** "Fairy tales!"
10. **Proof test:** something he must show and can't
11. **Verdict** (= the title)
12. **Believer's stake paid off**
13. **Ritual line + offer** (ad) / **choice + ritual** (organic)

## Gold standard (Case #1 "Breakfast", abridged)
> **Mae** *(her own plate is empty)*: Eat, Pip. Most important meal of the day.
> **Rennick:** On the house. Doctors' orders.
> **Crow:** Which doctors?
> **Rennick:** All of 'em. Forty-five hundred.
> **Crow:** Answering a letter from a campaign to sell bacon. Shh. Watch.
> **Crow:** Michigan, 1917. A health magazine prints the line. Its editor co-invented flaked cereal. *(1 date + 1 line + 1 image)*
> **Rennick:** My father fed this whole town on that plate!
> **Crow:** I know. I painted his sign. My best line.
> **Crow:** Read the question, Rennick. Heavy or light. Not one word about bacon. *(proof test)*
> **Pip:** So the plate was the ad.
> **Crow:** The most important meal was never a study. *(verdict = title)*
> **Mae:** Eat what you're hungry for. Take the rest for later. *(stake paid off)*
> **Crow:** You can eat because you're hungry. Or because someone wrote it down. Look again. *(ritual close)*

## Process
0. **Research gate (first, always):** competitor research → suggest 4 core drivers with evidence → **stop until the user approves** (`references/customer-drivers.md`).
1. **Topic:** product or trend (`references/trend-radar.md`).
2. **Myth:** a belief that expresses the driver, not trivia.
3. **One fact:** date + plain science line, verified online, source's exact verb ("first described", never upgraded to "invented").
4. **Concept:** one at a time → `next` / `approve` / `refine` / `kill`.
5. **Script** in the shape above.
6. **Principle check + read aloud:** each beat names its principle; beats with none are cut; if it sounds assembled or like a lecture, rewrite.
7. **Save** `projects/<NAME>/scripts/CaseNN_<item>_vN.md`. Never overwrite.

## Hard rules
- Stakes in line 1, paid off at the end.
- The narrator asks; he never accuses.
- One myth, one reveal, max 3 speakers.
- Every fact sourced, exact verb; in-story fiction stays clearly fiction.
- No promises about bodies or results; the verdict exposes the seller and never tells people what to do.
- 60s ad ≈ 150–165 words; organic 90–120s.
- Never copy a reference's lines, characters or look.
