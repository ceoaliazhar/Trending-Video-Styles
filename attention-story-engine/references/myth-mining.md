# Myth Mining: turn any topic into story angles

The reference engine needs ONE thing per video: **a belief people hold that has a hidden origin.** Mine the topic with these 8 lenses. Each lens produces 1–3 raw angles.

| # | Lens | Question to ask the topic | Reference example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Origin of the belief | Who first said this, and why? | 10,000 steps came from a 1965 pedometer ad |
| 2 | The number | Which famous number here was picked, not measured? | 10,000 steps · "8 glasses of water" |
| 3 | Who profits | Who makes money if people keep believing it? | Coffee growers named the "coffee break" |
| 4 | The invented ritual | Which habit/date/occasion was created by a seller? | Valentine's cards, Black Friday |
| 5 | The label vs reality | What does the label/name promise that the thing isn't? | "Made in Italy", "gives you wings" |
| 6 | The design trap | Which environment is built to steer behavior? | Supermarket layout, credit cards |
| 7 | Then vs now | What changed massively while one thing stayed frozen? | 1940 vs today: tools changed, 40-hour week didn't |
| 8 | Human nature lesson | Which emotion/behavior is everyone ashamed of but does? | Overthinking, flattery, envy |

> **Ads:** the myth must be the buyer's real fear from the drivers (L23). The lenses below are for organic topics.

## Procedure
1. Write the topic's **common beliefs** (10+), as people say them out loud ("Everybody knows…").
2. Run all 8 lenses → 10–20 raw angles.
3. For each angle, search: "<belief> origin history", "<number> who decided", "<habit> invented by". Keep only angles with a **dated, sourced fact**.
4. For each survivor, draft: myth line · verdict line · dateline · who profits/villain.
5. **Merge rule:** angles that share the same myth are NOT separate cards. Order them by date and turn them into the escalation ladder of one card (e.g. 1917 Kellogg magazine → 1922 Bernays physician survey = one "breakfast" card).
6. Send to scoring.

## Red flags (drop the angle)
- No primary/credible source for the twist.
- The "villain" is a real private individual.
- The flip requires a health/medical promise.
- The verdict would tell people what to do with their body/money ("skip breakfast", "stop taking X"). **The verdict exposes who sold the belief; it never prescribes behavior.** The close offers a choice, not an instruction.
- It's a conspiracy without evidence (we expose documented histories, not rumors).
